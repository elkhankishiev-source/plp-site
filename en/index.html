Yes. A foreigner takes a condominium unit in freehold — full ownership, forever — as long as the building's foreign quota is not exhausted (by law up to 49% of the floor area). Once the quota is full, the same unit is taken on leasehold, which is also a legitimate and workable route. Land is different: the plot is held on leasehold while the building itself is freehold, so the house is yours. We go through the structure for each specific property and show you exactly what you end up holding on paper.

Freehold is full ownership, available in condominiums within the foreign quota; such units are easier to resell. Leasehold is a long-term right for 30 years with renewals — it covers the land under a villa and units beyond the quota. A villa is usually structured as leasehold land plus freehold house. For investment people more often take a freehold condo; for living, a villa. The renewal terms are read by an independent lawyer before signing — we do not economise on that.

Transfer of title carries a 2% transfer fee on the assessed value. After that it depends on how long the seller has held the property: under five years — a 3.3% specific business tax, longer — 0.5% stamp duty; plus the seller's income tax. In practice the costs are split in half, but that is a matter of agreement and we handle the negotiation. Separately: a one-off sinking fund contribution, a year of common area fees paid upfront by some developers, and water and electricity connection.
